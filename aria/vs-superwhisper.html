Aria vs Superwhisper

Updated 15 October 2026 · 3 min read

Both tools run speech recognition locally on your Mac, so the privacy answer is similar. The difference is what surrounds the model. Choose Superwhisper-style tools when you want a raw, general-purpose transcription engine you control. Choose Aria Magic Voice when dictation should behave like typing — code-switching, direct injection into the app you are in, and a vocabulary that learns your terms.

Choose a Whisper-based tool if…

  • You regularly transcribe existing recordings — interviews, lectures, meeting audio — not just live dictation.
  • You want a raw transcription engine rather than a typing environment.
  • You are comfortable choosing and downloading a model and tuning it.
  • Your work is single-language, so code-switching does not matter to you.

Choose Aria if…

  • You write in two or three languages in the same document and switch without noticing.
  • You want dictation to land directly in the app you are already in, without copy-paste.
  • Your vocabulary is domain-specific and you want accuracy to improve over weeks of use.
  • You would rather not manage models — the correct model is already chosen and bundled.

On privacy, specifically

Local Whisper models and Aria both keep audio on your machine. If you are choosing between them, privacy is not the deciding factor — workflow fit is. If privacy is your deciding factor, verify it the same way for either tool: turn off the network and dictate. See how to test any dictation app.

Try both, in order

  1. Dictate the same mixed-language paragraph in both.
  2. Count corrections — especially invented words and missing punctuation.
  3. Dictate into a second app (mail, chat) and see whether either needs a paste step.
  4. Come back after a week and compare: which one's mistakes have gone down?
