Offline voice dictation on Mac: the short answer

Updated 15 October 2026 · 3 min read

Offline voice dictation on Mac is speech-to-text that runs entirely on your Mac — your audio is never uploaded, and the app keeps working with the Wi-Fi off. On a modern Mac, a local model can keep up with live dictation.

Three things decide whether a tool is genuinely "offline": (1) it works in airplane mode, (2) it never needs an account or a key, (3) it does not silently fall back to a cloud endpoint when your local model is unsure. Check all three — most tools that advertise "on-device" pass only the first.

What "offline" actually has to mean

Voice dictation tools describe themselves with three different words. They are not interchangeable, and the difference is the whole point if you dictate anything private.

TermWhere your audio goesWorks offline
On-device / localStays on your MacYes, always
HybridLocal first; cloud fallback for hard phrasesUsually — but the fallback is the catch
CloudUploaded to a server, returned as textNo

How to test any dictation app in 30 seconds

  1. Turn off Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. Dictate a full paragraph. Any error, spinner, or "check your connection" means it is not offline.
  2. Open Activity Monitor → Network. Dictate again. A local model shows a small, bursty transfer of nothing in particular; a cloud app shows a steady, repeating upload.
  3. Open a brand-new app (Notes, a blank doc). If you must manually aim a cursor or paste to get text in, it is not an input method — see the Aria overview.

What to look for beyond "offline"

  • Typing, not transcription. The best Mac dictation apps inject text directly into whatever app you are in — Slack, Notion, VS Code, Mail — instead of parking it on the clipboard.
  • Code-switching. If you write in both English and Chinese, a tool that forces a language switch mid-sentence is unusable in practice.
  • Learns your vocabulary. Product names, people's names, and jargon should get more accurate the more you dictate, not less.
  • Punctuation and filler cleanup built in. If it hands you a wall of unpunctuated text with "um" and "uh" still in it, you will edit more than you typed.
  • A price you can predict. One-time vs. subscription is a real fork: see Aria vs Apple Dictation.

FAQ

Does offline voice dictation on Mac work without internet?

Yes, if the app is genuinely on-device. Aria Magic Voice does not require a connection. Many "AI dictation" apps advertise local processing but silently route difficult audio to a server — airplane-mode testing is the only reliable check.

Is Apple Dictation offline?

Partly. macOS has long had on-device dictation available, but coverage, language support and accuracy depend on your system version and locale, so check System Settings › Keyboard › Dictation to see whether it is processed on your device. See the full comparison.

Which Mac dictation app works best for English + Chinese?

Code-switching is the deciding factor. Tools built for one language at a time force you to switch modes mid-sentence, which is where most bilingual dictation actually breaks. Aria is built for mixed English and Mandarin Chinese speech.

Do I need a subscription?

Not with Aria: Free tier available, Max is a one-time US$19.99 / ¥139 purchase. Some Whisper-based tools ask for a one-off payment, others bill monthly — check before you commit.

How fast is local speech-to-text on Apple Silicon?

On M-series Macs, On Apple Silicon Macs, on-device recognition is fast enough for live dictation. Exact speed depends on your Mac and settings.
